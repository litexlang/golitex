# Mathematical organization

Independence excludes each next vector from the span of its proper prefix:
a representation there would extend to a nontrivial zero combination of the
full list. During finite exchange, the already embedded prefix occupies
distinct generator indices. A next independent vector lies outside its prefix
span, so the existing outside-subspace theorem finds a nonzero coefficient
at a fresh index. Replacing that generator preserves the whole span.

Induction embeds the entire independent list into different positions of a
fixed-length generating list. Native finite injection/range cardinality then
proves the length bound. Applying it to two bases in both directions proves
equal lengths. Existing finite basis existence adds existence to that uniqueness.

The real consumer exercises an extracted basis with initially unspecified
length, rather than comparing two numerically equal constants. The zero-space
consumer preserves the natural zero length. There is no default basis, chosen
public dimension value or order requirement on the field.
