# Mathematical organization

A finite combination is divided into its selected scalar-vector contribution
and the remaining contributions. Coefficient addition and the sparse-value
theorem identify their sum. In the new span, the remaining value and the whole
represented value are members, so subspace cancellation recovers the selected
old generator when its coefficient is nonzero. Inclusion in the opposite
direction follows because every replacement generator was in the old span.

The nonzero-member corollary first finds a nonzero term outside the empty span,
constructs a replacement witness, and applies the criterion. The explicit
criterion allows the represented value to be zero. Requiring that the member
itself be nonzero is necessary only for the unconditional existential claim.

The plane example tests two distinct directions. The zero-coefficient negative
selects the unused direction in e1=1*e1+0*e2, which cannot guarantee equal spans.
The later basis_comparison export supplies a finite exchange induction and
length comparison; dimension selection and basis extension remain separate.
