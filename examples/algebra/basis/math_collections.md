# Mathematical organization

The generic proof first adds the negative of the right coefficient list to
both lists. Coefficient algebra shows the right sum has zero value; equal
original combination values make the left sum have zero value too. Independence
makes every coefficient of that difference zero. Scalar additive cancellation
then proves the lists equal by function extensionality, for arbitrary finite n.

Spanning separately supplies a coefficient representation for each vector.
Existence and independence-based uniqueness justify the selected coordinate
function. Its template guard requires the actual basis relation, which combines
independence and spanning. This is stronger than a supplied vector list or a
claim of finite-dimensionality, and it does not choose a basis automatically.

The real-line example proves those hypotheses for the unit list and computes
a coordinate. Named finite-sequence values and a checked entry fact bridge
projections of the returned coordinate list; an attempted deletion of those
bridges is preserved in the task journal. The singleton example validates the
zero-length boundary, keeping scalar zero distinct from scalar one while the
vector space contains only zero.

The shared prefix/deletion and length-two dependence results retain their
original domains and bounds. They do not imply general exchange or basis-length
invariance. These consumers establish a reusable coordinate interface conditional
on a supplied finite basis, while general dimension remains a separate proof
obligation. The broad undergraduate coverage goal remains active.
