# IMO 1972 — Problem 5

Let `f` and `g` be real-valued functions on the reals. Suppose `f` is not
identically zero, `abs(f(x)) <= 1` for every real `x`, and
`f(x + z) + f(x - z) = 2 * f(x) * g(z)` for every real `x,z`.
Prove `abs(g(y)) <= 1` for every real `y`.

The [Litex proof](imo_1972_p5.lit) expresses nonvanishing by the equivalent
hypothesis `exist a R st {f(a) != 0}` and takes arbitrary real `y` in the header.

Take the least upper bound `M` of the nonempty range of `abs(f)`. It is positive.
If `abs(g(y)) > 1`, the functional equation and triangle inequality make
`M / abs(g(y))` another upper bound of that range. Leastness would give
`M <= M / abs(g(y)) < M`, a contradiction.
