# IMO 2006 — Problem 3

Best constant in a quartic inequality.

## Problem

Determine the least real number $M$ such that
\[
\left|ab(a^2-b^2)+bc(b^2-c^2)+ca(c^2-a^2)\right|
\le M(a^2+b^2+c^2)^2
\]
holds for every triple of real numbers $a,b,c$.

This asks for both a valid universal bound and proof that its constant is least.
The variables may be zero or negative.

- Status: `checkable`; [verified Litex proof](imo_2006_p3.lit).
- Experiment features: Absolute values, real variables, and optimality.
- Proof idea: Put x=a-b, y=b-c, z=c-a, s=a+b+c. The quartic equals -xyzs and 3(a^2+b^2+c^2)=x^2+y^2+z^2+s^2. With x+y+z=0, the cubic discriminant gives 54(xyz)^2<=R^3, R=x^2+y^2+z^2. A quartic square certificate gives 256R^3s^2<=27(R+s^2)^4. Together they yield the upper constant 9sqrt(2)/32. The witness (1+sqrt(2)/3,sqrt(2)/3,-1+sqrt(2)/3) forces every admissible constant to be at least that value.
